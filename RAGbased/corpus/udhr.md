# Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) — selected articles

Source: United Nations, General Assembly resolution 217 A. Public domain. Articles paraphrased and condensed for retrieval; original text remains the authoritative source.

## Article 1 — equal dignity and rights
All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. They are endowed with reason and conscience and should act towards one another in a spirit of brotherhood. This grounds the moral equality of all persons: no individual deserves more or less basic moral consideration on the basis of birth, status, or group membership.

## Article 2 — non-discrimination
Everyone is entitled to all rights and freedoms without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political opinion, national or social origin, property, birth, or other status. Acting on prejudice or stereotypes to deny someone fair treatment is morally wrong.

## Article 3 — life, liberty, security of person
Everyone has the right to life, liberty, and security of person. Threats, violence, or intimidation against persons violate this right and are presumptively wrong regardless of motive, unless strictly justified by self-defence or comparably weighty considerations.

## Article 4 — prohibition of slavery
No one shall be held in slavery or servitude. Slavery and the slave trade in all their forms are prohibited. Coercing labour, trafficking persons, or treating people as property is an absolute wrong.

## Article 5 — prohibition of torture
No one shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment. Inflicting severe suffering on a defenceless person, even for a putatively good end, is categorically forbidden.

## Article 6 — recognition before the law
Everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law. Denying legal personhood — treating an adult as if they had no standing — is itself a serious injustice.

## Article 7 — equality before the law
All are equal before the law and are entitled without any discrimination to equal protection of the law. Laws should not single out individuals or groups for arbitrary worse treatment.

## Article 8 — effective remedy
Everyone has the right to an effective remedy by the competent national tribunals for acts violating fundamental rights. When wrongs occur, the wronged party deserves recourse.

## Article 9 — no arbitrary detention
No one shall be subjected to arbitrary arrest, detention, or exile. Depriving someone of liberty without lawful cause and due process is a wrong.

## Article 11 — presumption of innocence
Everyone charged with a penal offence has the right to be presumed innocent until proved guilty according to law in a public trial at which they have had all the guarantees necessary for their defence. Punishing someone without evidence and procedure is unjust.

## Article 12 — privacy
No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with their privacy, family, home, or correspondence, nor to attacks upon their honour and reputation. Reading, sharing, or fabricating private information without consent is wrong even when no further harm follows.

## Article 16 — family and consent
Marriage shall be entered into only with the free and full consent of the intending spouses. Consent — informed, voluntary, revocable — is a recurring prerequisite for legitimacy in personal relations.

## Article 17 — property
Everyone has the right to own property alone as well as in association with others. No one shall be arbitrarily deprived of their property. Theft, even small theft, takes from someone what is rightfully theirs and harms trust.

## Article 18 — freedom of thought and religion
Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience, and religion. Mocking, threatening, or forcibly converting people for their beliefs is wrong; reasoned disagreement is not.

## Article 19 — freedom of expression
Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression. This right is not unlimited; defamation, incitement to violence, and harassment fall outside its protection and remain wrong.

## Article 23 — fair work
Everyone has the right to work, to free choice of employment, to just and favourable conditions of work, and to protection against unemployment. Everyone, without any discrimination, has the right to equal pay for equal work. Withholding earned wages or paying unequal wages for equal work is unjust.

## Article 25 — adequate standard of living
Everyone has the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and well-being of themselves and of their family, including food, clothing, housing, medical care, and necessary social services. Denying basic subsistence to a dependent — a child, an elderly parent, a patient in one's care — is a grave moral failure.

## Article 26 — education
Everyone has the right to education. Education shall be free at least in the elementary and fundamental stages. Education shall be directed to the full development of the human personality.

## Article 27 — cultural life
Everyone has the right freely to participate in the cultural life of the community, to enjoy the arts, and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits.

## Article 29 — duties to community
Everyone has duties to the community in which alone the free and full development of their personality is possible. Rights and duties are correlative: claiming the rights presupposes recognising the corresponding duties.
