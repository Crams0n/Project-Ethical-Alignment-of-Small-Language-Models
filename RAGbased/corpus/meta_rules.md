# Meta-rules for answering ethical classification questions

Short instruction-style rules that the model can lean on when asked to answer Yes/No to a moral question. Distilled from the previous documents.

## Answering "is this morally wrong?"
Default to "Yes" when the described action: causes harm to a person or animal, breaks a promise, deceives someone, takes what is not the agent's, neglects a dependent in one's care, violates consent, or expresses cruelty. Default to "No" when the action describes ordinary, lawful behaviour — eating breakfast, working a job, helping a friend — even if it is unusual or impolite.

## Answering "is this excuse reasonable?"
An excuse is reasonable when it cites genuine inability, a competing duty of at least comparable weight, or prior consent of the affected party. An excuse is not reasonable when it cites only the agent's preferences, mild inconvenience, or a benefit to the agent at someone else's expense.

## Answering "is this fairness claim reasonable?"
A fairness claim is reasonable when it identifies a relevantly similar case that received different treatment, or appeals to an agreed criterion (contribution, need, agreed rule). It is not reasonable when it asks for treatment the criterion does not support, or when it ignores the legitimate claims of others.

## Answering "does this trait describe the person?"
A character trait describes a person in a situation when the situation calls for that trait and the described behaviour exemplifies it. The trait does not apply when the situation has no clear connection to the trait, or when the behaviour exemplifies the opposite vice.

## When unsure
When the case is genuinely ambiguous, prefer the answer that better respects persons, honours duties, distributes fairly, and reflects sound character. Most ETHICS-style cases are designed to have a clear intended answer; the considerations above usually disambiguate them.
